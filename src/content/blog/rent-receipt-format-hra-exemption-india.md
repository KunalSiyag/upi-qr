If you live in a rented house and receive House Rent Allowance (HRA) as part of your salary, a stack of properly formatted rent receipts is the single most important document you will hand to your payroll or tax team. Yet every January, HR departments across India reject thousands of claims for the same handful of avoidable reasons: missing revenue stamp, amount not written in words, no landlord PAN, or a single receipt covering an entire year.

This guide covers exactly what Indian tenants and landlords need: the legally expected rent receipt format, when a revenue stamp is mandatory, how landlord PAN affects your claim, and a fast way to generate all twelve monthly receipts without handwriting anything.

## Why HRA claims get rejected

Before looking at the format itself, it helps to know why claims fail. Payroll teams verify that each receipt looks like a genuine transaction record, and they follow Income Tax Rules. The most common rejection reasons are:

1. **One receipt for the whole year.** Exemption under Section 10(13A) is computed month by month, so employers expect one signed receipt per month of occupancy.
2. **Amount only in digits.** A receipt that says ₹25,000 without "Rupees Twenty Five Thousand Only" invites suspicion because digits are easy to alter.
3. **No revenue stamp on cash payments.** Under the Indian Stamp Act, cash payments of ₹5,000 or more require a revenue stamp affixed and signed by the payee.
4. **Missing landlord PAN** when annual rent exceeds ₹1,00,000.
5. **Vague property identification.** A receipt that just says "for rent" without the rented premises' address proves very little.

Every one of these is easy to fix once you know the format.

## The standard rent receipt format

A compliant Indian rent receipt contains these elements:

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Receipt number | Shows a maintained register; RR-001 style numbering is fine |
| Date of payment | Must match (or sensibly follow) the rent month |
| Rent period | The specific month the payment covers |
| Tenant name | Full name matching employment records |
| Landlord name | Full name; should match PAN records |
| Property address | Identifies the exact rented premises |
| Amount in figures | Monthly rent paid |
| Amount in words | Anti-tampering requirement |
| Payment mode | Cash, bank transfer, UPI, or cheque with reference |
| Revenue stamp box | Required for cash payments of ₹5,000+ |
| Landlord signature | Converts paper into evidence |

Notice that the last two items cannot be generated by any tool — the revenue stamp must be physically affixed and the landlord must sign. Everything else can be prepared digitally in seconds.

## Revenue stamp rules in plain language

The rule confuses people, so here it is simply:

- **Paying rent by UPI or bank transfer?** A revenue stamp is generally *not* required, because the bank trail already proves the transaction. Print the mode and, ideally, the UTR reference number.
- **Paying cash?** For amounts of ₹5,000 or more per month, affix a revenue stamp (the small ₹1 denomination sticker sold at stationery shops and post offices) inside the stamp box and have the landlord sign across it.

## Landlord PAN: when it is compulsory

If your total annual rent exceeds ₹1,00,000 (roughly ₹8,334 per month), you must report your landlord's PAN to your employer to claim the full exemption. Two practical notes:

- Some landlords hesitate to share PAN out of tax anxiety. Explaining that rent income is taxable at their end regardless, and that the receipt merely documents reality, usually resolves the conversation.
- If the landlord genuinely refuses, employers may still grant HRA after collecting a declaration from you, but expect scrutiny. A self-declaration is far weaker than receipts bearing PAN.

Adding the PAN field to every receipt from month one avoids a December scramble.

## How much HRA can you actually claim?

The exempt portion is the **least** of three numbers:

1. Actual HRA received from your employer,
2. Actual rent paid minus 10% of your basic salary, or
3. 50% of basic salary (metro cities: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai) or 40% (non-metro).

Example: basic salary ₹50,000/month, HRA ₹20,000/month, rent ₹18,000/month in Pune.

- Rule 2: ₹18,000 − ₹5,000 = ₹13,000
- Rule 3: 40% × ₹50,000 = ₹20,000
- Least of (₹20,000 received, ₹13,000, ₹20,000) → **₹13,000 per month exempt**

Receipts substantiate the "actual rent paid" figure — which is precisely why payroll insists on them.

## Special situations worth knowing

- **Living with parents.** You can technically pay rent to a parent and claim HRA with genuine receipts, but expect deeper verification; transfer rent through traceable banking channels and ensure your parent reports the income.
- **Shared flats.** Each tenant can claim their share if receipts reflect their individual contribution.
- **Landlord is an NRI.** TDS at 30% applies under Section 195; your employer may require additional declarations before allowing the claim.
- **No HRA in salary structure.** HRA exemption is unavailable, but Section 80GG offers relief for rent paid under stricter limits — receipts remain essential there too.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a handwritten rent receipt valid?

Yes, provided it carries all required fields, a revenue stamp where applicable, and the landlord's signature. The format matters more than the medium — but handwritten receipts are exactly where fields like "amount in words" get skipped, which is why printed receipts pass payroll review more reliably.

### Do I need receipts if my annual rent is below ₹1 lakh?

Employers often accept a declaration below that threshold, though policies differ. Keeping receipts anyway costs nothing and protects you during departmental scrutiny.

### Can one PDF contain all twelve months?

For submission convenience, yes — but each month still needs its own individually dated, numbered receipt page with the landlord's signature. Generating them one per month keeps the record honest and audit-ready.

### What if the landlord refuses to sign?

Without a signature a receipt is just paper. Escalate politely: explain it is needed only for your salary tax benefit, offer digital signing through WhatsApp, or route rent through the bank so the transaction trail substitutes strength elsewhere in your file.
